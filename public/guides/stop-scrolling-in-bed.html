How to Stop Scrolling in Bed

You are tired, your judgement is thin, and nobody is watching. It is the worst possible moment to be relying on willpower — which is exactly what most people do.

6 min read · Updated August 2026

Of all the screen time habits, phone-in-bed is the one people fail at most consistently. Not because it is the strongest habit, but because it happens at the hour when every defence you have is at its lowest.

You are tired. Your judgement is degraded. There is no next appointment forcing you to stop, and nobody will see. Any plan that depends on you making a good decision at 11:40pm is a plan that has already failed.

Why the usual advice underperforms

"Charge your phone in another room" is genuinely the most effective single change, and it works — right up until the night you bring it in anyway, which requires no tools and no passcode. It is a good habit with no enforcement.

Night Shift and warm-tone filters address blue light, which is a real but modest part of the problem. The larger issue is engagement, not colour temperature. A warm-toned feed is still a feed.

"Just put it down" is the plan you have already tried every night this year.

The specific difficulty is that the decision to stop has to be made by the version of you least equipped to make it.

A setup that survives the hour

1. Start the block before you are tired

Set it to begin at 9pm, not 11. A rule that starts after you are already in bed is a rule you will negotiate with. A rule that started two hours ago is simply the state of the world.

This is the single highest-leverage adjustment, and almost everyone gets it wrong by setting the window too late and too narrow.

2. Block the category, and include the surprises

Social is obvious. The ones that catch people are news, YouTube, shopping, and email — email especially, because it feels productive and is the most common trojan horse into a scrolling session.

3. Make it something you cannot lift in bed

This is the crux. At 11:40pm you will not be talked out of it by a reminder screen, and a five-minute wait is just five minutes of lying there before you get what you wanted.

The setup that holds is one where getting back in requires someone else. In Disconnect that is Hard lock — no override, and only your buddy can approve. The practical beauty of it at this hour is that your buddy is probably asleep, so the request simply goes unanswered and the block stays on. The silence is the feature.

4. Keep the alarm, lose the excuse

"I need it for my alarm" is the reason most people give for the phone being in the room. Blocking apps rather than the device leaves the clock, alarm and phone calls working normally, which removes the excuse without removing the safety.

A block that holds at midnight

Hard lock cannot be lifted from your own phone. If your buddy is asleep, nothing unlocks — which at 1am is exactly what you wanted.

Give the hour something else to be

A block removes the option but does not fill the gap, and an empty gap at bedtime tends to get filled by whatever is nearest. Decide in advance what replaces it — a physical book, a paper notebook, anything that is not a screen and is already within reach.

Put the replacement on the nightstand before the block starts. Deciding what to do instead, at the moment you cannot do the thing, is another decision made by the wrong version of you.

What the first fortnight looks like

Expect the first week to be uncomfortable and the second week to be worse — that is when you will find yourself genuinely annoyed and looking for the loophole. It passes. Most people report the reaching stops around week three, and the thing that surprises them is not better sleep but how much longer the evening feels.

If you are sending unlock requests most nights in week four, widen the window rather than tightening the lock. The rule is usually starting too late.

Common questions

Quick answers.

How do I stop using my phone in bed?

Start the block well before bedtime rather than at bedtime, block the whole category including news and email, and use a lock you cannot lift yourself at that hour. Blocking apps rather than the device leaves your alarm and calls working.

Does keeping my phone out of the bedroom work?

It is the most effective single change, but it has no enforcement — nothing stops you bringing it in. It works best combined with an app block that holds even when the phone is beside you.

What if I need my phone as an alarm?

Block apps rather than the device. Your clock, alarm and phone calls keep working normally, which removes the most common reason people give for having an unrestricted phone within reach.

What time should I start a bedtime app block?

Earlier than feels necessary — usually about two hours before you intend to sleep. Relapse happens in the window just before a rule starts, so a block beginning at 9pm holds far better than one beginning at 11pm.
