How to Block Social Media on iPhone (And Actually Keep It Blocked)

Every guide tells you how to set the block. Almost none tell you why it will be gone by Thursday, or what to do about that.

By Shuaib Khan · 7 min read · Updated August 2026

Setting a social media block on an iPhone is genuinely easy. Apple ships the tools, they work, and you can be done in about two minutes. Here they are, briefly, before the part that actually matters.

The three ways to block it

Apple Screen Time

Settings → Screen Time → App Limits → Add Limit → Social. Set a daily allowance, or set it to one minute to make the apps effectively unusable. Turn on Block at End of Limit, or the limit is only a suggestion.

Free, built in, and works across every social app at once by category.

Delete the apps, keep the accounts

Removing the app while leaving the account intact raises the friction without losing anything. Most people find the mobile web versions annoying enough that this alone cuts usage sharply.

A third-party blocker

Apps like Disconnect, Opal, one sec and ScreenZen all block by app or category on a schedule, with different degrees of strictness. Which one you need depends on how you actually fail.

Now the real problem

Every method above shares a flaw: you can undo all of them in under thirty seconds. The Screen Time passcode is yours. The App Store reinstall is two taps. The blocker has a settings screen.

So the block does not fail on day one. It fails on day nine, at 11pm, when you tap Ignore Limit for a reason that feels completely legitimate — and then never turn it back on.

The block does not need to survive you when you are calm. It needs to survive you when you are not.

What makes a social media block stick

1. Block the category, not the app

Blocking Instagram alone moves the habit to TikTok within a week. Every serious tool lets you block a whole category at once. Use that, or you will spend a month playing whack-a-mole with your own attention.

2. Set the window wider than the problem

If you lose evenings from 9pm, start the block at 8. Relapse happens in the half-hour before the rule takes effect, when you know it is coming and get one last session in.

3. Remove the off switch from your own hands

This is the one that decides the outcome. A block you can lift is a block you will lift. The two setups that hold are having someone else set a passcode you never see, or using an app where another person approves each unlock remotely.

Disconnect's Hard lock is the second kind: no override, no backdoor, and only your buddy can approve getting back in. It is in the free version, along with the gentler levels for the times you do not need something that strict.

4. Keep one deliberate way in

A block with no escape hatch gets deleted the first time you genuinely need the app for something real. What you want is an escape hatch that costs something — a request, a short wait, another person's yes — rather than a toggle.

A setup that survives the first month

  1. Pick the category, not one app. Social, or whichever grouping matches your problem.
  2. Choose the level honestly. If you have already broken your own limit on these apps, a reminder will not hold. Go straight to the strictest option.
  3. Schedule it wide, covering the hour before your danger window.
  4. Leave a deliberate exception for the genuine cases, so you never need to dismantle the whole thing.
  5. Do not adjust it for two weeks. Editing a rule during a craving is unlocking with extra steps.

What to expect

The first few days are easy. Week two is where you will feel actively annoyed and start negotiating with yourself — that is the system working, and it is where most people quit. By week three or four the reaching mostly stops on its own, because some part of you has registered that the door is genuinely shut.

If you are still fighting it daily in week four, the rule is usually wrong rather than your resolve. Narrow the window or cut the number of apps and try again.

Common questions

Quick answers.

How do I block social media apps on my iPhone?

The built-in route is Settings, Screen Time, App Limits, then add a limit on the Social category and enable Block at End of Limit. You can also delete the apps while keeping your accounts, or use a third-party blocker that works on a schedule.

How do I block social media so I can't unblock it?

Any block whose passcode or settings you control can be lifted by you. The setups that genuinely hold are having another person set a code you never see, or using an app with a built-in approval flow where a trusted person approves each unlock.

Should I block one app or the whole category?

The whole category. Blocking a single app usually just moves the habit to the next one within about a week, so category-level blocking is the only version that changes total usage.

Why do my screen time limits stop working after a week?

Because you hold the passcode, so the limit asks you for permission at the moment you are least able to refuse. Most people do not consciously quit — they unlock once for a real reason and never re-enable it.
